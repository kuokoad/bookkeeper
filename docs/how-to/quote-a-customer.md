# Quote a customer

A contractor asks what 200 bags of cement and 40 lengths of pipe will cost. They
are asking two other yards the same question. What they want is a piece of paper
with your name on it, a price, and a date the price is good until.

That is a quotation. It sells nothing.

## Write one

**Quotations → New quote.**

- **Customer.** Type any name. They do not have to be on your customer list, and
  most people asking for a price are not on it yet. If they already are, start
  typing and pick them.
- **Job or site.** Optional, and worth filling in. "Adenta site" is how you find
  this quote again in three weeks when they ring back and cannot remember the
  number.
- **Valid until.** Filled in a month ahead. Change it to suit the job. This date
  does real work — see below.
- **Items.** Choose the product, put in the quantity, and adjust the price.
  Prices start at what is on your shelf and you can go under it, because quoting
  is negotiating and a yard that could only quote list price would win nothing.
- **Discount on the whole quote.** For when you knock something off the total
  rather than off one line.

Save it. The quote gets a number like `QTE-00001`.

## Give it to them

**Print** on the quote gives you an A4 sheet with your shop's name, logo,
address and phone at the top, the items and the total, and the date the price is
good until. It says plainly that it is a quotation and not a bill, so nobody
mistakes it for something they owe you.

You can also choose **Save as PDF** in the print dialog and send it on WhatsApp.

## Nothing has been sold

This is the part worth being sure of. A quote:

- posts nothing to your books
- moves no stock
- **reserves nothing** — quoting 200 bags does not hold 200 bags, so if somebody
  walks in tomorrow and buys them, they are gone

Your profit, your stock and your balances all read exactly the same before and
after you write a quote. That is deliberate. Until the customer says yes,
nothing has happened.

## Change it

Contractors change their minds, usually twice. **Change** on an open quote lets
you edit anything on it. The quote number stays the same, because the customer
may be holding a piece of paper with that number on it.

Once a quote has become a sale, or been cancelled, it can no longer be changed.

## When they accept

**The customer accepted**, on the quote.

Choose where the money goes, and how much they are paying now. If they are
paying only part of it, you will be asked to add them to your customers, because
what is still owed has to be owed by somebody.

Press **Turn into a sale**. From there it is an ordinary sale: stock leaves, the
books are posted, and there is a receipt to print. The quote closes and links to
the sale it became, and the sale links back.

**The prices on the quote are what the customer pays**, even if you have put
your shelf prices up since. That is what a quote is for.

A quote can only become a sale once. Convert it twice and the second attempt is
refused — otherwise the goods would go out twice off one piece of paper, and
both sales would look perfectly normal afterwards.

## When the date has passed

Cement moves. A quote written in March and honoured in August can sell stock
below what it now costs you to replace, and nothing on the screen would look
wrong.

So an expired quote is not simply converted. You are asked **why it is still
being honoured**, and whatever you type is kept with the quote and in the audit
log. It is one sentence, and it is the difference between a decision you made
and a price that slipped through.

The quote list shows expired ones as **Ran out**, and there is a quick filter
for them.

## When they go elsewhere

**Cancel this quote**, with a reason. Nothing is deleted: the quote stays on
file, keeps its number, and can still be printed. A price your shop once offered
is worth being able to look up.

## Who can do this

Quotations are their own area in **Users**, and a new staff account does not get
it. Quoting is where margin is given away, and a price promised for thirty days
is the owner's decision. Tick it for a senior hand if you want to, the same way
you would tick Sales or Purchases.

## If you cannot see Quotations at all

Then your shop type does not use them. **Settings → What kind of business is
this?** A building materials yard has quoting switched on, a general retail shop
does not, and you can turn the feature on or off underneath whichever type you
chose. Switching it off hides the menu; it removes no quote you have written.
